Lane County homelessness-response spending, CY2023–2025

Research note. Posted 2026-11-13. Dataset: lane_county_housing_response_rollup.csv.

What this is

A rollup of Lane County voucher-register payments to homelessness-response and housing-assistance program lines for calendar years 2023–2025. Built to make one number checkable: how much the county spends managing the housing shortage after the fact.

Source & method

  • Source: Lane County voucher payment register covering CY2023–2025, produced under the Oregon Public Records Law (ORS 192.311 et seq.).
  • Normalization: payments keyed to the county's own expenditure account codes and program labels; no reclassification of the county's accounting.
  • Scope: program lines in the county's Human Services housing/homelessness block (codes 3427060–3427070), plus the Oregon Rehousing Initiative (3427032), Long-Term Rental Assistance (3427038), the Statewide Shelter Program (3427040), Governor's Executive Order 23-02 homelessness-response funding (3427076), and Housing & Community Development (5152070).
  • Figures are gross voucher payments. Much of this spending is restricted state and federal pass-through money administered by the county; it is not all discretionary county money. About $0.6M of payments carry no parseable date in the register export and are counted in the total only, not in the per-year figures.

Findings

  • Total, CY2023–2025: $46,955,415 across 16 program lines and 5,745 payments.
  • By year: 2023 — $5.6M · 2024 — $22.1M · 2025 — $18.7M (undated — $0.6M). The 2024 jump reflects Executive Order 23-02 shelter-capacity funding arriving in the register.
  • Largest program lines: Executive Order 23-02 — $14.5M; Continuum of Care — $8.1M; Prevention & Diversion — $6.8M; Statewide Shelter Program — $4.4M; Long-Term Rental Assistance — $4.2M; Emergency Housing Account — $2.2M; Oregon Rehousing Initiative — $1.9M.

What this does and does not show

This note establishes the scale of downstream-response spending — shelter, rent assistance, rehousing, and homelessness services. It does not show waste: most lines are restricted pass-through programs performing triage that the housing shortage makes necessary, and most cannot legally be redirected to construction. It does not include staff costs paid outside the voucher register, budgeted (versus actual) amounts, or spending by cities, the housing authority, or nonprofits from other funding streams.

Data

lane_county_housing_response_rollup.csv — aggregate totals by program line and year. We publish the aggregate rather than the row-level register to limit republishing of individual payee names; the underlying register is a public record obtainable from Lane County.
