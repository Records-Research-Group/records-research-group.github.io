F1 — Transparency-dead-zone deflection

Claim
When asked for payment-level records, desks point requesters at the state transparency portal knowing — or without checking — that the portal does not carry the requested record class.
Stated obligation
ORS 192.324: the custodian must respond to the request as made; a referral to a public dataset satisfies it only if the dataset actually contains the records.
Observed behavior
At least three desks (OWEB, the Chiropractic Examiners board, DEQ) responded to the identical vendor-payments ask by pointing to the Oregon Transparency "State Agency expenditures" dataset. We verified that dataset (data.oregon.gov y9g9-xsxs): vendor × expenditure-class × fiscal-year aggregates only, FY2019–FY2025 — no payment-level rows, no transaction dates, no document numbers, no current-year data. When the gap was sent back to the Chiropractic Examiners board in writing (2026-11-12), the desk produced a full payment-level A811 AP export — 1,024 rows, FY2024–FY2027 — five days later, no charge.
Witnesses
RRG-30, RRG-96, RRG-35. The post-rebuttal production is in the ledger at RRG-30.
What this does not establish
Whether the redirect is policy, script, or individual habit; whether the desks knew the dataset's coverage limits. It establishes only that the referral was verifiably empty — and that the real export existed and could be produced within days once the referral was shown to be empty.
Status
Documented; production after documented rebuttal is the observed correction path.

F2 — Person-payee exclusion from published feeds

Claim
At least one agency's published payment data systematically omits payments to person payees while retaining vendor payees — the class of payee determines whether a payment is publicly legible.
Stated obligation
No statute requires the proactive feed; the finding bears on whether the published layer can substitute for the requested records.
Observed behavior
Springfield's published payment data contains vendor payments but no person-payee rows, while the ledger confirms person payees are paid through the same system.
Witnesses
RRG-14 / RRG-15 (Springfield) in the scoreboard.
What this does not establish
The mechanism — exclusion may be a privacy rule, a feed-spec choice, or an export artifact. Pending the feed specification we cannot say which.
Status
Documented; mechanism open pending feed spec.

F3 — Portal↔ledger divergence ($1.32M)

Claim
A published transparency total and the agency's own disbursement ledger for the same period diverged by approximately $1.32M — decomposable to specific missing record classes.
Stated obligation
Same as F2 — no proactive-feed duty, but the divergence means the published figure cannot stand in for the underlying records.
Observed behavior
The divergence was decomposed into missing classes rather than left as an unexplained delta; it is not attributable to rounding or coverage-window effects.
Witnesses
Lane County disbursement thread, RRG-16.
What this does not establish
That the portal figure is wrong — only that it and the ledger cannot both be complete for the same scope. Which side omits what is documented at the record-class level.
Status
Documented.

F4 — Desk-level fee treatment, including narrowing-immune estimates

Claim
For the same statutory fee authority and near-identical asks, desks produced outcomes ranging from free full exports to four-figure estimates — and at least two estimates were structured so that reducing the request's scope does not reduce the price.
Stated obligation
ORS 192.324(4): fees must be reasonably calculated to reimburse actual costs of making records available. ORS 192.324(5): fees shall be reduced or waived where furnishing is in the public interest — an individualized determination.
Observed behavior
  • Lane County Clerk #26-974: $728.89 estimate for aggregate annual counts. After a narrowing request, the county's estimate-defense (2026-11-13) priced it as "existence research" — 12 clerk-hours plus paralegal and attorney review — a price the scope reduction cannot touch. 60-day pay-or-close under ORS 192.329(3)(b).
  • Lane County Clerk #26-975: after a second scope reduction, the county's written reply (2026-11-17) stated "the fee estimate has not changed" — same immunity, on the record.
  • PUC: $132.50 for three years or $88 for an already-prepared narrower file; prepayment required per OAR 860-001-0060(3); the billing/acceptance step requests name, company, address, and phone.
  • OSBEELS: opened with a PDF request form demanding name, address, phone, and signature; after an email-channel reply declining the form, the demand was dropped and the desk produced QuickBooks vendor exports — 1,363 payment-level rows, ~$7.12M, FY2024–FY2026 — at no charge (2026-11-18).
  • OWRD produced 14,264 payment-level rows and DSL a disbursement export at no charge — the same record class priced at $0 and at $728.89 by different desks in the same period.
  • Waiver record: 23 waivers/reductions requested under ORS 192.324(5); 2 granted, 5 denied (denials cited the requester's "plan and ability to distribute the information" under the In Defense of Animals v. OHSU discretionary standard), remainder pending or mooted by free production.
Witnesses
RRG-10 / #26-974; RRG-21 / #26-975; RRG-94 (PUC); RRG-76 (OSBEELS); RRG-66 (DSL); RRG-64 (OWRD); RRG-17 / #26-971.
What this does not establish
That any single estimate exceeds the county's actual costs — the county's math is internally plausible. The finding is that identical asks price differently by desk, and that "existence research" pricing makes the fee structurally immune to the requester's statutory narrowing option.
Status
Documented; both narrowing-immunity instances are under petition (L3, L7) with the Lane County DA.

F5 — Email channel accepted in law, refused at desks

Claim
Oregon law accepts a written email as a public-records request; whether it is processed as one depends on which desk receives it.
Stated obligation
ORS 192.311 et seq. defines a public records request as a written request — an emailed request is a written request. No statute requires portal submission.
Observed behavior
  • Four bodies — ODHS, DOC, OPRD, DPSST — categorically refused to process emailed requests and directed the requester to portal-only channels. All four refusals are under petition (L1, L5, L6, L7).
  • The Criminal Justice Commission replied (2026-11-16) that a reference number exists only via the oregon.gov web form; staff then pasted our email into that form themselves, generating auto-confirmation Record ID 38629 — the request was valid all along; the portal was for the workflow, not the record.
  • Lane County and City of Eugene both re-enter emailed requests into their own portals (NextRequest tickets 26-1032/26-1042/26-1055/26-1083; JustFOIA PRR-2026-896/926/948) — processing the request while boilerplate-steering the requester to the portal for "next time." The channel-switch letter is itself part of the record.
Witnesses
RRG-93 (DPSST); RRG-71 (CJC portal-relay); #26-1055 and #26-1083 (channel-switch letters); PRR-2026-926.
What this does not establish
That portals exist to obstruct — they carry real workflow value for the agency and produce ticket numbers the requester can cite. The finding is that the portal demand is a desk posture, not a legal requirement, and collapses on the desks' own re-filing behavior.
Status
Documented; the four hard refusals are petitioned.

F6 — Delivery mechanics as friction

Claim
Even where records are released, the mechanics of delivery — image-only PDFs, link-only portal notices, portal-download-required — can reduce or eliminate the record's usability.
Observed behavior
Deliveries observed include a scanned image-only PDF (no text layer), portal notices containing no records (documents must be fetched inside the portal session), and portal-bound files requiring an account the requester declined to create.
Witnesses
RRG-93, RRG-49, RRG-152.
What this does not establish
Intent — each mechanism is defensible on IT/security grounds. This is the weakest of the published findings and is kept for completeness.
Status
Documented (weakest).

F7 — Mass nonresponse; the petition layer is itself silent

Claim
One-third of the identical wave-2 state requests were never acknowledged at all — past the statutory acknowledgment window — and the escalation venue the statute provides has so far produced zero orders, including on petitions whose own 7-day order clocks have lapsed.
Stated obligation
ORS 192.324(2): acknowledge within five business days. ORS 192.415/192.418: on petition, the DA (county) or Attorney General (state) shall issue an order within seven days.
Observed behavior
53 of 163 sent requests were logged as nonresponses past the acknowledgment window on 2026-11-20. Eighteen petitions for review have been filed (L1–L18): channel refusals (L1, L5, L6, L7), constructive denial via narrowing-immune fees (L3, L7), a fee-waiver denial (L4), a batch of categorical nonresponses (L8–L17), and a sheriff in-custody-deaths request (L18). The earliest petitions' statutory 7-day order clocks have lapsed; as of 2026-11-23 no order has been received on any petition. The oversight layer is exhibiting the same nonresponse behavior the petitions document.
Witnesses
Scoreboard rows carrying "Nonresponse past ORS 192.324 ack window" status (53 rows); petition filings noted on RRG-10, RRG-21, RRG-93, and the L8–L17 batch rows.
What this does not establish
That nonresponses are deliberate — a share of any 130-desk sweep will be address rot, spam filtering, or staffing. The finding is the distribution: silence is the modal outcome for unwatched requests, and the petition remedy — the statute's own counter-pressure — currently imposes no observable cost either.
Status
Documented; order clocks remain open. Each lapsed clock is itself a scorecard datum.

F8 — Custodianship seams: where responsibility is nobody's

Claim
The hardest failures are not refusals but vacuums: subjects where a physical or administrative reality exists and no entity is obligated to hold records for it — so every desk's "no responsive records" is individually true and collectively damning.
Stated obligation
ORS 192 obligates custodians of existing records; it cannot obligate a custodian for records nobody was charged with keeping.
Observed behavior
The Millrace diversion dam — a low-head structure on Eugene's millrace with a documented history of fatalities — was asked about at three desks. OWRD's dam-safety program confirmed low-head dams are not state-regulated: no statutory dam named Millrace in Lane County, no inventory or file; an email search found zero OWRD↔City/EWEB correspondence on any dam-name variant. EWEB's records custodian disclaimed that the utility ever owned the canal or diversion dam — a liability-framed answer, not a records answer. The City request is in flight. The structure exists and periodically kills people; in the records system it has no custodian. A related pattern: nine of nine "rollup" agencies — boards whose spending runs through a parent department — answered the vendor-payments ask "no responsive records," even though the spending is public under the parent's code. Each answer may be individually accurate; the class of records is unreachable through the desks that should own it. A contrasting instance shows the working resolution path: Lane County's released ESS contract set appeared to skip Amendment #2 and four Exhibit C revisions; when asked under ticket 26-1083 the county certified the released set complete — the gaps were unexecuted numbering designations, verifiable against the released documents' own replaces-chain. Apparent custody gaps can be numbering conventions; only asking distinguishes them.
Witnesses
RRG-154 (EWEB), RRG-155 (OWRD), RRG-153 (City, in flight); rollup no-records rows e.g. RRG-80, RRG-81; #26-1032 and #26-1083 (ESS set — resolved numbering gap).
What this does not establish
That records were destroyed or that any desk's absence claim is false — "no responsive records" is unfalsifiable from outside, and that unfalsifiability is itself part of the finding: the places custody cannot be audited are exactly where responsibility was diffused enough to leave no custodian.
Status
Documented; City response pending.
