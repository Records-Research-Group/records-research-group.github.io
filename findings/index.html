F1 — Transparency-dead-zone deflection

Claim
When asked for payment-level records, desks point requesters at the state transparency portal, which does not carry the requested record class — making the referral verifiably non-responsive to the ask.
Stated obligation
ORS 192.324: the custodian must respond to the request as made; a referral to a public dataset satisfies it only if the dataset actually contains the records.
Observed behavior
Two desks (OWEB, the Chiropractic Examiners board) are documented in quoted correspondence pointing a payment-level ask to the Oregon Transparency "State Agency expenditures" dataset; a third (DEQ) is ledger-logged without reproduced correspondence. We verified that dataset (data.oregon.gov y9g9-xsxs): vendor × expenditure-class × fiscal-year aggregates only, FY2019–FY2025 — no payment-level rows, no transaction dates, no document numbers, no current-year data. When the gap was sent back to the Chiropractic Examiners board in writing (2026-11-12), the desk produced a full payment-level A811 AP export — 1,024 rows, FY2024–FY2027 — five days later, no charge.
Witnesses
RRG-30, RRG-96, RRG-35. The post-rebuttal production is in the ledger at RRG-30.
What this does not establish
Whether the redirect is policy, script, or individual habit; whether the desks knew the dataset's coverage limits; a mistaken good-faith referral remains possible. It establishes only that the referral was verifiably non-equivalent to the ask — and that the real export existed and could be produced within days once the mismatch was explained.
Status
Documented; production after documented rebuttal is the observed correction path.

F2 — Person-payee exclusion from the state transparency portal

Claim
Reported: payee-level exports produced by two Oregon agencies contain payments to named individual persons that do not appear on the state transparency portal, while reimbursements to the same agencies' own employees do appear. The underlying diff dataset is not yet published.
Stated obligation
No statute requires the proactive feed; the finding bears on whether the published layer can substitute for the requested records.
Observed behavior
In OWRD's produced FY2025 detail, 208 payees totaling ~$1.08M — water-program grant and award payments to named individuals — have no portal presence in any year. In DSL's FY2025 detail, 16 payees totaling $205,619 — sole-proprietor contractors — are likewise absent. A third produced agency (OSBGE) has no portal presence at all; a fourth (DOGAMI) shows a different gap shape — purchase-order obligations rather than payments — and does not replicate the pattern.
Witnesses
RRG-64 (OWRD, 14,264 rows) and RRG-66 (DSL), each diffed against Socrata API queries of the portal's vendor universe, all years — re-runnable by diffing payee name sets between production and portal. OSBGE/DOGAMI: RRG-122, RRG-78.
What this does not establish
That individuals are deliberately suppressed for privacy — employee names paid as vendors do publish. The measured pattern is consistent with an instrument-class or filer-status scope decision in the portal feed; the precise mechanism is knowable only from the feed specification, which is itself a public record.
Status
Reported — matching used exact payee-name set difference against the portal's Socrata API vendor universe; alternate-name handling is documented in the study ledger. Mechanism open pending the feed specification; the diff dataset is in preparation for publication.

F3 — Portal and ledger diverge by $1.32M (FY2025, OWRD)

Claim
For OWRD FY2025, the transparency portal reports $32,666,271.83 while the agency's own produced vendor-payments export totals $33,982,830.31 — a $1,316,558.48 divergence that decomposes into three measured components at displayed precision.
Stated obligation
No statute requires the proactive feed; the finding bears on whether the published layer can substitute for the requested records.
What was compared, and what explains it
Portal total for OWRD, fiscal year ended 2025-06-30, vs. the payment-level export OWRD produced under ORS 192 for the same period. The divergence resolves as:
  • −$5.29M portal-side only: 22 payees appearing on the portal but not in the export — all reported as other state agencies (DAS $2.72M, DOJ $2.09M, etc.), consistent with inter-agency transfers counted as portal expenditure but excluded from a vendor-payments export.
  • +$4.12M production-side only: 246 payees in the export but absent from the portal — reported as fiscal-boundary obligations and the F2 person-payee class.
  • +$2.49M same-vendor year disagreement: 131 shared vendors where the two surfaces report different FY2025 totals — consistent with appropriation-year vs. transaction-date bucketing, not yet verified at transaction level.
Differences in coverage and fiscal-year treatment are competing explanations consistent with the observed totals; their contribution has not yet been verified at transaction level. The discrepancy alone does not establish that either total is wrong.
Witnesses
RRG-64 (OWRD production) vs. portal API aggregates. Verification limitation: the source totals are reproduced (the two aggregate figures and the rounded components), but the per-vendor difference table and input extracts are maintained in the study ledger and not yet published — component assignments and the fiscal-year explanations are researcher-reported.
What this does not establish
That either figure is incorrect — the finding is that the two public surfaces are not reconcilable by a member of the public without the detailed export, which is what a records request is for — and which the F1-style portal referral would leave undone.
Status
Arithmetic verified; component assignments researcher-reported pending publication of the reconciliation inputs.

F4 — Desk-level fee treatment, including narrowing-immune estimates

Claim
Under the same statutory fee authority, desks produced outcomes ranging from free full exports to four-figure estimates — and at least two estimates were structured so that reducing the request's scope did not reduce the price.
Stated obligation
ORS 192.324(4): fees must be reasonably calculated to reimburse actual costs of making records available. ORS 192.324(5): fees shall be reduced or waived where furnishing is in the public interest — an individualized determination.
Observed behavior
  • Lane County Clerk #26-974: $728.89 estimate for aggregate annual counts. After a narrowing request, the county's estimate-defense (2026-11-13) priced 12 clerk-hours as research "to find and compile" records, plus paralegal coordination and attorney exemption review — a price the specific scope reduction did not reduce (our shorthand: "existence research" pricing). 60-day pay-or-close under ORS 192.329(3)(b).
  • Lane County Clerk #26-975: after a second scope reduction, the county's written reply (2026-11-17) stated "the fee estimate has not changed" — same immunity, on the record.
  • PUC: ~$132 for three years or $88 for a one-year narrowed file; prepayment required per OAR 860-001-0060(3); the billing/acceptance step requests name, company, address, and phone.
  • OSBEELS: opened with a PDF request form demanding name, address, phone, and signature; after an email-channel reply declining the form, the demand was dropped and the desk produced QuickBooks vendor exports — 1,363 payment-level rows, ~$7.12M, FY2024–FY2026 — at no charge (2026-11-18).
  • OWRD produced 14,264 payment-level rows and DSL a disbursement export at no charge, while the Clerk priced an aggregate-counts request at $728.89 — different asks and record classes; the comparison demonstrates fee variance across desks, not like-for-like pricing of an identical record.
  • Waiver record: 23 waiver/reduction requests across the corpus, including federal FOIA rows; 2 granted — one under ORS, one under federal FOIA — and 5 denied (the denial letters in hand cite the requester's "plan and ability to distribute the information" under the In Defense of Animals v. OHSU discretionary standard), remainder pending or mooted by free production.
Witnesses
RRG-10 / #26-974; RRG-21 / #26-975; RRG-94 (PUC); RRG-76 (OSBEELS); RRG-66 (DSL); RRG-64 (OWRD); RRG-17 / #26-971.
What this does not establish
That any single estimate exceeds the county's actual costs — the county's math is internally plausible, or that no narrowing could ever move an estimate — the two documented reductions did not. The finding is fee variance across desks and estimates priced on "find and compile" labor that the tested scope reductions did not touch.
Status
Documented; both narrowing-immune estimates are under petition (L3, L7-975) with the Lane County DA.

F5 — Email channel accepted in law, refused at desks

Claim
Oregon law accepts a written email as a public-records request; whether it is processed as one depends on which desk receives it.
Stated obligation
ORS 192.311 et seq. defines a public records request as a written request — an emailed request is a written request. No statute requires portal submission.
Observed behavior
  • Four bodies — ODHS, DOC, OPRD, DPSST — are logged as categorically refusing emailed requests and directing the requester to portal-only channels; verbatim refusal text is held for DOC ("we do not accept requests via email") and DPSST ("all records requests must be submitted through GovQA"). All four refusals are under petition (L1, L5, L6, L7-DPSST).
  • The Criminal Justice Commission replied (2026-11-16) that a reference number exists only via the oregon.gov web form; staff then pasted our email into that form themselves, generating auto-confirmation Record ID 38629 — the email was sufficient to docket the request; the portal was for the workflow, not the record.
  • Lane County and City of Eugene both re-enter emailed requests into their own portals (NextRequest tickets 26-1032/26-1042/26-1055/26-1083; JustFOIA PRR-2026-896 — the city's own letter confirms the courtesy entry; companion tickets PRR-2026-926 and PRR-2026-948 were filed directly through JustFOIA per the portal auto-confirmations, so they are portal-filing data points, not relay witnesses) — processing the request while boilerplate-steering the requester to the portal for "next time." The channel-switch letter is itself part of the record.
Witnesses
RRG-93 (DPSST); RRG-71 (CJC portal-relay); #26-1055 and #26-1083 (channel-switch letters); PRR-2026-926.
What this does not establish
That portals exist to obstruct — they carry real workflow value for the agency and produce ticket numbers the requester can cite. The finding is that the portal demand is a desk posture, not a legal requirement — agency-side re-filing is an observed alternative to requiring requester resubmission.
Status
Documented; the four hard refusals are petitioned.

F6 — Delivery mechanics as friction

Claim
Even where records are released, the mechanics of delivery — image-only PDFs, link-only portal notices — can reduce the record's immediate usability or add retrieval steps.
Observed behavior
Eugene's PRR-2026-896 payroll production (RRG-13) arrived as a scanned image-only PDF — no text layer — where a usable export had been requested; the completion notice contained a link rather than the records themselves (the documents were publicly retrievable at the posted link — no account was required). A GovQA auto-acknowledgment at DOC (RRG-49) shows the same channel mechanics at intake.
Witnesses
RRG-13 / PRR-2026-896; RRG-49 (GovQA auto-ack).
What this does not establish
Intent — delivery-format choices may be defensible on IT or security grounds; that rationale is not established by the correspondence. This is the weakest of the published findings and is kept for completeness.
Status
Documented (weakest).

F7 — Mass nonresponse; the petition layer is itself silent

Claim
50 of the 108 identical wave-2 state request rows (46%) were never acknowledged at all — past the statutory acknowledgment window — and the escalation venue the statute provides has so far produced zero orders, including on petitions whose own 7-day order clocks have lapsed.
Stated obligation
ORS 192.324(2): acknowledge within five business days. ORS 192.415/192.418: on petition, the DA (county) or Attorney General (state) shall issue an order within seven days.
Observed behavior
51 rows are labeled nonresponsive as of 2026-11-23 — 50 of the 108 canonical state request rows (46%; desk-level 49 of 106 after deduplicating two agencies the enumeration sent twice), plus one sheriff in-custody-deaths request. Eighteen petition identifiers are logged as filed (L1–L18, 19 filings — L7 denotes two distinct petitions: L7-DPSST at the AG and L7-975 at the Lane DA): channel refusals (L1, L5, L6, L7-DPSST), constructive denial via narrowing-immune fees (L3, L7-975), a fee-waiver denial (L4), a batch of categorical nonresponses (L8–L17), and the sheriff request (L18). Filing dates are ledger-logged from send records; transmission receipts and any orders are the authoritative evidence. The earliest petitions' statutory 7-day order clocks have lapsed; as of 2026-11-23 no order has been received on any petition — a narrower observation than "the oversight layer did nothing."
Witnesses
Scoreboard rows carrying a nonresponsive status (51 rows); petition filings noted on RRG-10, RRG-21, RRG-93, and the L8–L17 batch rows.
What this does not establish
That nonresponses are deliberate — a share of any ~130-desk sweep will be address rot, spam filtering, or staffing. The finding is the count and the elapsed clocks: nearly half the identical canonical asks past the acknowledgment window, and zero orders received on petitions whose statutory clocks have run.
Status
Documented; order clocks remain open. Each lapsed clock is itself a scorecard datum.

F8 — Custodianship seams: custody not yet located

Claim
The responses received so far have not identified a custodian for the requested Millrace records: OWRD reports no responsive records, EWEB disclaims ownership and records for the specified items, and the City's response remains pending.
Stated obligation
ORS 192 obligates custodians of existing records; whether any entity bears recordkeeping duties for this structure is not resolved by the responses to date.
Observed behavior
The Millrace diversion dam — a functioning low-head structure on Eugene's millrace — was asked about at three desks. OWRD's dam-safety program confirmed low-head dams are not state-regulated: no statutory dam named Millrace in Lane County, no inventory or file; an email search found zero OWRD↔City/EWEB correspondence on any dam-name variant. EWEB's records custodian responded that the utility "does not own and has never owned" the canal or diversion dam and stated no responsive records for several items. The City request is in flight. The structure exists; among the desks asked so far, no custodian has been identified. A related pattern, researcher-reported: nine of nine "rollup" agencies — boards whose spending runs through a parent department — answered the vendor-payments ask "no responsive records," even though the spending is public under the parent's code. Each answer may be individually accurate; the nine underlying replies and parent-code evidence are summarized in the ledger, not reproduced here. A contrasting instance shows the working resolution path: Lane County's released ESS contract set appeared to skip Amendment #2 and four Exhibit C revisions; when asked under ticket 26-1083 the county certified the released set complete — the gaps were unexecuted numbering designations, verifiable against the released documents' own replaces-chain. Apparent custody gaps can be numbering conventions; only asking distinguishes them.
Witnesses
RRG-154 (EWEB), RRG-155 (OWRD), RRG-153 (City, in flight); rollup no-records rows e.g. RRG-80, RRG-81; #26-1032 and #26-1083 (ESS set — resolved numbering gap).
What this does not establish
That records were destroyed or that any desk's absence claim is false — a "no responsive records" answer is not independently tested here, though contradictory records could refute it. Nor does it establish that no entity holds recordkeeping obligations — custody may exist at a desk not yet asked; the City response is pending.
Status
Documented; City response pending.

Scope, selection & comparability

How bodies were selected. Wave 1 (2026-10-26) was a Lane County pilot — county offices, Eugene, Springfield, and state-local desks, chosen for proximity and subject relevance; its asks differed per agency by design. Wave 2 (2026-11-04/05) enumerated Oregon state bodies from the Secretary of State's Blue Book State Agencies, Boards and Commissions list plus the Governor's Board Book: 143 entries after folding subunits to parents, including zero-budget boards ("no disbursements" is itself a response class); excluded: judiciary, legislative bodies, and staff-less advisory task forces. Wave 3 sent a FOIA variant to federal bodies with Oregon presence — 16 canonical vendor-FOIA rows appear in the ledger (judiciary, congressional offices, USPS, and DOD-no-installation excluded from selection).

What is comparable. Wave-2 and wave-3 rows carry one canonical ask — payment-level vendor records, FY2024 + FY2025 + FYTD, fee waiver cited — and outcomes are directly comparable within each legal regime: same request, different desks. Wave-1 rows are heterogeneous asks and should not be pooled with the canonical rows when comparing outcomes; fee and response figures from wave 1 are illustrative of their own threads, not controls. Federal rows run under FOIA (5 U.S.C. §552, 20-business-day determination, federal FY ending September 30) — a different statute and clock; they are published in the same ledger but are not part of the ORS rates.

What the rates mean. Figures like "50 of 108 canonical request rows unacknowledged (46%; 49 of 106 distinct desks)" describe this corpus — one requester, identical asks, a defined denominator — not the rate at which all requesters or all requests are ignored. The instrument measures desk behavior under an email-channel requester posture; portal-first requesters may see different treatment, and a share of nonresponses will trace to address rot, spam filtering, or staffing rather than posture. Where a finding covers a small number of bodies (F1: two verified + one ledger-logged; F2: two replicating, one counter-example) the count is stated with it. Two agencies received the canonical ask twice under enumeration spelling variants (OGEC rows 25/50, ODHS rows 24/41) — desk-level figures deduplicate them.

Subject response rights

Every body named in a finding was contacted on the underlying request thread — several findings document the reply itself changing the outcome (a produced export after a documented rebuttal; a next-business-day answer that resolved a document-set question). Beyond that record: any body named in a finding may submit a response at any time via the contact address below; substantive responses are appended to the finding verbatim and dated. Factual corrections receive the dated treatment described on the about page.

Method note

Every datum above is traceable to the published request ledger or a per-request page, except F2/F3's analytical inputs (diff dataset, reconciliation table), which are marked researcher-reported pending publication; correspondence metadata (dates, quoted text) is maintained in the internal evidence archive and summarized on the request pages. Statuses are a dated snapshot — the ledger shows each row's state as of 2026-11-23 and updates as correspondence arrives. Internal findings not yet meeting the verification bar are withheld from this page rather than published in softened form.

Corrections policy: factual errors receive a dated correction line on the affected page and a note in the feed. Contact via the about page.
